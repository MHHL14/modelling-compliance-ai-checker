## 1 Purpose

1.1 This Policy sets out the principles under which the Bank takes, measures, approves, mitigates and monitors credit risk. It establishes the credit risk appetite framework, the system of credit decision authority, the requirements for rating assignment and for the use of models in credit decisions, the rules for overriding model outcomes, the principles for collateral, the fair treatment of customers in credit processes and the monitoring of credit exposures.

1.2 The Policy gives effect to the requirements on credit risk management in Directive 2013/36/EU (CRD, in particular Article 79), to the IRB use requirements of Regulation (EU) No 575/2013 (CRR, in particular Articles 144, 172 and 173), to EBA/GL/2020/06 on loan origination and monitoring and to EBA/GL/2017/06 on credit risk management practices.

## 2 Scope and applicability

2.1 The Policy applies to the Bank and all its consolidated subsidiaries (together, the Bank) and to all activities that give rise to credit risk, including lending, guarantees and other off-balance-sheet commitments, and the credit risk component of treasury activities in the banking book.

2.2 The Policy applies to all models used in credit processes, including rating systems, acceptance scorecards, pricing models, limit-setting models, early-warning models and provisioning models, irrespective of whether they are used for own funds requirements.

2.3 Subsidiaries shall adopt local credit risk policies that are consistent with this Policy. Local deviations require a dispensation under section 12.

2.4 Detailed requirements are set out in subordinate standards, in particular CRM-STD-100 (IRB rating systems), CR-STD-110 (definition of default), CR-STD-120 (credit acceptance and scorecards) and FIN-STD-200 (IFRS 9 expected credit loss).

## 3 Definitions

- **Credit risk appetite** — the level and types of credit risk the Bank is willing to assume in pursuit of its strategy, expressed in the Risk Appetite Statement.
- **Credit decision** — any decision to grant, increase, extend, restructure or materially amend a credit facility, including the setting of limits and conditions.
- **Delegated authority** — the authority, conferred personally on an officer or a committee, to take credit decisions up to specified limits.
- **Rating** — the assignment of an obligor or exposure to a grade or pool of an approved rating system, with an associated probability of default (PD).
- **Override** — a rating or credit decision that deviates from the outcome produced by the approved model or rule set.
- **Protected attribute** — a personal characteristic on the basis of which discrimination is prohibited under EU or national law, including nationality, gender, ethnic or racial origin, religion or belief, disability, sexual orientation and age.
- **Proxy variable** — a variable that is not itself a protected attribute but is associated with a protected attribute to an extent that its use could produce indirectly discriminatory outcomes.
- **Watchlist** — the list of performing obligors under enhanced monitoring because of identified signs of credit deterioration (section 10).
- **Default** — default as defined in CR-STD-110.

## 4 Credit risk appetite

4.1 The Management Board shall set the credit risk appetite at least annually as part of the Group Risk Appetite Statement, which is approved by the Supervisory Board.

4.2 The credit risk appetite shall be expressed in quantitative metrics with limits and early-warning triggers, covering as a minimum: expected loss and cost of risk; risk-weighted asset density; single-name, sector and country concentration; the distribution of exposures over rating grades; the non-performing exposure ratio; and, for new residential mortgage production, the distribution by loan-to-value and debt-service-to-income.

4.3 The credit risk appetite shall be cascaded to business lines and portfolios through limits. A breach of an early-warning trigger shall be reported to the Group Risk Committee at its next meeting; a limit breach shall be reported within five business days together with a remediation proposal.

4.4 Risk parameters used to measure appetite metrics (PD, LGD, EAD, expected credit loss) shall be produced by models that are registered in the model inventory and approved in accordance with the Model Risk Management Policy (MRM-POL-001).

4.5 The credit risk appetite shall be assessed against the results of the Bank stress tests carried out in accordance with ERM-STD-300.

## 5 Credit decision authority

5.1 All credit decisions shall be taken under delegated authority. The delegated authority matrix is approved by the Management Board and sets limits by total exposure to the group of connected clients and by rating grade.

5.2 Credit decisions outside fully automated processes shall follow the four-eyes principle: a proposal by the commercial function and an independent approval by Credit Risk Management for all decisions above the business-line authority threshold.

5.3 Fully automated credit decisions are permitted only for retail products and only within the policy rules, cut-offs and affordability parameters approved in accordance with CR-STD-120. The automated decision process shall itself be approved by the Retail Credit Committee.

5.4 Credit decisions exceeding the highest individual authority shall be taken by the Group Credit Committee. Exposures to a group of connected clients exceeding 10% of the Bank's Tier 1 capital require approval by the Management Board.

5.5 Every credit decision shall be documented, including the rating, the rationale, the approved limits, conditions and covenants, and the collateral taken.

5.6 Officers and committee members with a conflict of interest in relation to a credit decision shall abstain from the decision; the abstention shall be recorded.

## 6 Rating assignment and use of models in credit decisions

6.1 No credit decision shall be taken without a valid rating of the obligor (non-retail) or a valid pool or grade assignment (retail) produced by the approved rating system for the portfolio.

6.2 Rating systems used for exposures under the IRB approach shall comply with CRM-STD-100 and CRR Articles 169 to 173.

6.3 Non-retail ratings shall be reviewed at least annually, and at least semi-annually for obligors on the watchlist. A rating older than 12 months is expired; no new credit may be granted to an obligor with an expired rating. Retail behavioural ratings shall be refreshed at least monthly.

6.4 Non-retail rating assignments shall be completed or approved by a party in Credit Risk Management that does not benefit directly from the decision to extend the credit, in accordance with CRR Article 173(1)(b).

6.5 Models used in credit decisions (rating, acceptance, pricing, limit-setting) shall be approved and independently validated before use in accordance with MRM-POL-001, and shall be used only within their approved scope of application.

6.6 IRB ratings and risk parameters shall play an essential role in credit approval, limit setting, risk-adjusted pricing, internal capital allocation, provisioning and management reporting, as required by CRR Article 144(1)(b). Where a different measure is used for an internal purpose, the difference shall be documented and justified.

6.7 Known model limitations, conditions of approval and compensating controls shall be communicated to the users of the model in credit decision processes.

## 7 Rating overrides

7.1 Overrides of model outcomes are permitted only on the basis of relevant information that is not captured by the model. The admissible override reasons shall be defined per rating system in a closed list, in accordance with CRR Article 172(3).

7.2 Every override shall be documented with the reason code, a written rationale, the proposer and the approver. Overrides shall be approved by Credit Risk Management independently of the proposer.

7.3 Upward overrides of non-retail ratings shall not exceed two notches on the Bank master scale unless approved by the Group Credit Committee.

7.4 Override rates shall be monitored per rating system at least quarterly. An override rate above 10% of rating assignments for non-retail rating systems, or above 5% of decisions for retail rating and acceptance systems, shall trigger an analysis by the Model Owner and shall be reported to Model Risk Management as input to model monitoring under MRM-STD-040.

7.5 The performance of overridden ratings shall be analysed at least annually by comparing the default rates of overridden and non-overridden assignments per grade. Overrides that are systematically in the same direction for the same reason shall be treated as an indication of a model deficiency.

7.6 Overrides in retail acceptance processes are governed by CR-STD-120.

## 8 Collateral and credit risk mitigation

8.1 Credit shall be granted on the basis of the obligor's capacity to meet its obligations from its primary source of repayment. Collateral is a secondary source of repayment and shall not by itself justify a credit decision.

8.2 Collateral shall be legally enforceable in all relevant jurisdictions and documented so as to ensure legal certainty, consistent with CRR Article 194(1).

8.3 Immovable property shall be valued by an independent, qualified valuer at origination. Values shall be monitored at least once a year for commercial immovable property and at least once every three years for residential property, using statistical indexation in the intervening years, in accordance with CRR Article 208(3).

8.4 Collateral data (type, value, valuation date and method, lien rank and link to the secured facilities) shall be recorded in the collateral management system and shall meet the data quality requirements of DATA-POL-001 and MRM-STD-022.

8.5 The recognition of collateral in own LGD estimates shall follow CRM-STD-100 and EBA/GL/2020/05.

## 9 Fair treatment of customers

9.1 The Bank treats its customers fairly and does not discriminate, directly or indirectly, on the basis of protected attributes in credit decisions, pricing or rating assignment.

9.2 Protected attributes (e.g. nationality, gender, ethnicity) shall be excluded from credit models as explanatory variables, segmentation criteria and override reasons. Age may be used only where legally permitted and objectively justified by the credit characteristics of the product (for example remaining term to retirement in affordability assessment), and such use shall be approved by Credit Risk Management and documented.

9.3 Every candidate variable of a credit model shall be screened for its potential to act as a proxy for a protected attribute. Screening shall combine a qualitative assessment with, where lawfully available, a quantitative analysis of association with protected attributes. Postcode, place of birth, first name, language preference and similar variables are presumed potential proxies.

9.4 A variable identified as a potential proxy may be used only where a legitimate credit-risk rationale is documented and its use is restricted to the minimum needed, for example aggregation of postcode to regional level. The restriction and its rationale shall be documented in the model documentation.

9.5 Credit models that are used in decisions on natural persons shall be tested for disparate outcomes across groups defined by protected attributes before approval and at least annually thereafter, using the metrics and thresholds of AI-STD-700 and processing personal data in accordance with PRIV-POL-001.

9.6 The results of the proxy screening and outcome testing shall be documented in the reference data set and model development documentation and reviewed by Model Validation. A model with material unexplained disparities shall not be approved for use.

9.7 Applicants whose application is declined shall be informed of the decision and of the main reasons in understandable terms. Where a decision is based solely on automated processing, the applicant's right to obtain human intervention under GDPR Article 22 shall be ensured.

9.8 Models used to evaluate the creditworthiness of natural persons that qualify as AI systems are high-risk AI systems under Annex III of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and are subject to the Responsible AI Policy (AI-POL-001).

## 10 Credit monitoring

10.1 Every portfolio shall have documented early-warning indicators, drawn from payment behaviour, financial information, external data and market information. Obligors that trigger early-warning indicators shall be assessed within 30 days and, where credit deterioration is confirmed, placed on the watchlist.

10.2 Non-retail credit files shall be reviewed at least annually, covering financial performance, covenant compliance, collateral values and the rating.

10.3 Credit Risk Management shall report to the Group Risk Committee at least quarterly on appetite metrics, rating migration, concentrations, watchlist, forborne and non-performing exposures, and override rates.

10.4 Defaults shall be identified in accordance with CR-STD-110. Defaulted non-retail obligors shall be transferred to Financial Restructuring and Recovery.

10.5 Impairment shall be determined in accordance with FIN-STD-200. Stage 3 exposures to obligors with a total exposure of EUR 1 million or more are assessed individually; all other Stage 3 exposures are assessed collectively.

10.6 The performance of credit models shall be monitored in accordance with MRM-STD-040. Credit Risk Management shall receive the monitoring results of all models used in credit decisions.

## 11 Roles and responsibilities

11.1 **Management Board** — sets the credit risk appetite and the delegated authority matrix and approves this Policy.
11.2 **Group Risk Committee** — oversees the credit risk profile against appetite and recommends this Policy for approval.
11.3 **Group Credit Committee** — takes credit decisions above individual authorities and approves material overrides.
11.4 **Credit Risk Management (2LoD)** — owns this Policy, independently approves credit decisions and ratings, and monitors the portfolio.
11.5 **Business lines (1LoD)** — originate and manage credit exposures and are accountable for the risks they take.
11.6 **Credit Risk Modelling** — develops and maintains credit models as Model Owner or on behalf of the Model Owner.
11.7 **Model Risk Management and Model Validation** — perform the functions set out in MRM-POL-001 and MV-STD-001.
11.8 **Internal Audit (3LoD)** — assesses the effectiveness of the credit risk framework at least every three years.

## 12 Exceptions and dispensations

12.1 Deviations from this Policy require a dispensation approved by the Chief Risk Officer, time-bound to a maximum of 12 months and recorded in the policy dispensation register, which is reported to the Group Risk Committee quarterly.

12.2 No dispensation may be granted from the exclusion of protected attributes under 9.2.
