## 1 Purpose

1.1 This policy sets the Bank's framework for identifying, assessing, managing and monitoring risks arising from arrangements with third parties, including outsourcing arrangements and the use of ICT services provided by ICT third-party service providers.

1.2 The policy implements the EBA Guidelines on outsourcing arrangements (EBA/GL/2019/02) and the ICT third-party risk requirements of the Digital Operational Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, DORA), in particular Articles 28 to 30. It is consistent with the Model Risk Management Policy (MRM-POL-001) for models, data and AI systems obtained from third parties.

## 2 Scope and applicability

2.1 This policy applies to all entities of the Bank and to every arrangement under which a third party provides a service, function, product or data to the Bank, including intragroup arrangements.

2.2 It covers in particular: outsourcing of functions; ICT services, including cloud services; externally developed models, model components and calibrations; external data used in models; and AI systems and general-purpose AI models provided by third parties, including models accessed via an interface.

2.3 Where a third-party arrangement involves a model or AI system, this policy applies in addition to MRM-POL-001, AI-POL-001 and, for generative AI, AI-STD-710. Use of a vendor model does not reduce the Bank's responsibility for understanding, validating and monitoring that model.

## 3 Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Outsourcing | An arrangement under which a service provider performs a process, service or activity that would otherwise be performed by the Bank itself (EBA/GL/2019/02). |
| ICT third-party service provider | An undertaking providing ICT services as defined in Art. 3 DORA. |
| Critical or important function | A function whose disruption would materially impair the Bank's financial performance, soundness or continuity of services, or its compliance with authorisation conditions (Art. 3 DORA; EBA/GL/2019/02 section 4). |
| Register of Information | The Bank's register of all contractual arrangements on the use of ICT services, maintained under Art. 28(3) DORA, integrated with the outsourcing register required by EBA/GL/2019/02 section 11. |
| Arrangement Owner | The business manager accountable for a third-party arrangement throughout its lifecycle. |
| Exit strategy | A documented plan to terminate an arrangement and transfer the service to another provider or back in-house without undue disruption. |

## 4 Governance

4.1 The Management Board retains full responsibility for all outsourced functions and third-party services. Responsibility cannot be outsourced.

4.2 Operational Risk owns this policy and operates the Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM) framework as second line of defence.

4.3 Every third-party arrangement shall have a named Arrangement Owner in the first line of defence.

4.4 The Management Board shall adopt, and review at least annually, a strategy on ICT third-party risk as part of the digital operational resilience strategy, including a policy on the use of ICT services supporting critical or important functions (Art. 28(2) DORA).

4.5 Arrangements shall be proportionate to the nature, scale and complexity of the risks involved. The depth of due diligence, the contractual protections and the intensity of monitoring increase with the criticality of the function supported and the materiality of the data processed.

## 5 Pre-contract assessment and due diligence

5.1 Before entering into an arrangement, the Arrangement Owner shall assess whether the arrangement constitutes outsourcing and whether it supports a critical or important function. The criticality assessment shall be documented and reviewed by TPRM.

5.2 Before entering into an arrangement, the Arrangement Owner shall identify and assess all relevant risks, including operational, ICT and security, legal, compliance, reputational, conflict-of-interest, data protection and concentration risks.

5.3 Due diligence on the prospective provider shall assess its business reputation, financial soundness, technical and organisational capability, information security standards, business continuity arrangements, sub-contracting chain, location of service delivery and data processing, and compliance with applicable law.

5.4 For providers of models, AI systems or general-purpose AI models, due diligence shall in addition cover: the documentation available on methodology, training data provenance and known limitations; evaluation and performance evidence; the provider's change and versioning practices; terms on the use of the Bank's data by the provider; and the information the Bank needs to comply with MRM-POL-001 and the AI Act.

5.5 Potential conflicts of interest arising from the arrangement, including intragroup arrangements and arrangements with providers in which the Bank or its staff hold interests, shall be identified, assessed and managed before contract signature.

5.6 The Bank shall only enter into arrangements with ICT third-party service providers that comply with appropriate information security standards (Art. 28(5) DORA).

5.7 For arrangements supporting a critical or important function, the Bank shall inform the competent authority in a timely manner of the planned arrangement (Art. 28(3) DORA; EBA/GL/2019/02).

## 6 Contractual requirements

6.1 Every arrangement shall be documented in a written contract. For ICT services, the contract shall include at least the elements of Art. 30(2) DORA, including a description of services, locations of service provision and data processing, data protection provisions, access, recovery and return of data, service levels, assistance in ICT incidents, cooperation with competent authorities, and termination rights.

6.2 For arrangements supporting a critical or important function, the contract shall in addition include the elements of Art. 30(3) DORA and EBA/GL/2019/02 section 13, including quantitative service levels, notice and reporting obligations, business contingency plans and their testing, unrestricted rights of access, inspection and audit for the Bank and the competent authority, and exit provisions with a mandatory adequate transition period.

6.3 Contracts for models, AI systems or data used in models shall grant the Bank the right to receive sufficient documentation to understand and independently validate the product, advance notification of material changes, and access to performance information.

6.4 Sub-outsourcing of critical or important functions shall be permitted only under conditions specified in the contract, including prior notification and the right of the Bank to object or terminate.

## 7 Register of Information

7.1 Every third-party arrangement within scope shall be recorded in the Register of Information before the service commences.

7.2 The Register of Information shall be maintained at entity and consolidated level in the format required by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2956 and shall be updated within ten business days of any change to an arrangement.

7.3 The Register of Information shall be reported to the competent authority as requested and at least annually (Art. 28(3) DORA).

7.4 Where an arrangement supports a model or AI system, the Register of Information entry shall reference the Model Inventory or AI Inventory identifier, and vice versa.

## 8 Concentration risk

8.1 Before entering into an arrangement supporting a critical or important function, the Bank shall assess whether the arrangement would lead to contracting a provider that is not easily substitutable or to multiple arrangements with the same or closely connected providers (Art. 29 DORA).

8.2 TPRM shall monitor concentration at provider level quarterly, including concentration of AI and cloud services, and report material concentrations to the Non-Financial Risk Committee.

## 9 Ongoing monitoring

9.1 The Arrangement Owner shall monitor the performance of each arrangement against contractual service levels and key risk indicators, at a frequency based on criticality and at least annually.

9.2 Arrangements supporting critical or important functions shall be subject to a full risk reassessment at least annually and on material change, and to audits at a frequency based on the risk assessment (Art. 28(6) DORA).

9.3 For models and AI systems from third parties, ongoing monitoring shall include monitoring of model performance in the Bank's environment under MRM-STD-040 and assessment of provider-notified changes before they are applied in production.

9.4 The Bank shall be able to terminate an arrangement where the provider breaches applicable law or contractual terms, where circumstances are identified that could alter the performance of the service, where weaknesses in the provider's ICT risk management are evident, or where the competent authority can no longer effectively supervise the Bank as a result of the arrangement (Art. 28(7) DORA).

9.5 Incidents at third parties affecting the Bank's services shall be reported to TPRM without undue delay and classified under the Bank's ICT incident process (Art. 17–19 DORA).

## 10 Exit strategies

10.1 For every arrangement supporting a critical or important function, the Bank shall have a documented exit strategy covering termination scenarios, alternative solutions, transition plans, data retrieval and the time and resources required (Art. 28(8) DORA; EBA/GL/2019/02 section 15).

10.2 Exit strategies shall be tested for feasibility at least every three years and reviewed on material change.

10.3 For models and AI systems provided by third parties, the exit strategy shall identify a fallback approach, such as an alternative provider, an in-house model or a manual process, and the conditions under which it is activated.

## 11 Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Management Board | Approves this policy and the ICT third-party risk strategy; remains accountable for outsourced functions. |
| Non-Financial Risk Committee | Oversees third-party risk profile and concentration risk. |
| Arrangement Owner (1LoD) | Performs criticality assessment, due diligence, monitoring and exit planning. |
| TPRM, Operational Risk (2LoD) | Owns the framework and the Register of Information; challenges assessments. |
| Procurement and Legal | Ensure contracts meet section 6. |
| Model Risk Management | Advises on arrangements involving models; ensures inventory linkage. |
| Internal Audit (3LoD) | Reviews the framework and critical arrangements. |

## 12 Exceptions and dispensations

12.1 Exceptions require approval by the Non-Financial Risk Committee, are limited to twelve months and are recorded in the Register of Information. No exception may be granted to requirements imposed by DORA or by the competent authority.
