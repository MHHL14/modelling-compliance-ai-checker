## 1 Purpose

1.1 This Policy sets the Bank's framework for identifying, measuring, managing, monitoring and reporting climate-related and environmental (C&E) risks. It defines how C&E risks are assessed for materiality, integrated into credit risk management, the internal capital adequacy assessment process (ICAAP) and stress testing, how the required data are sourced, and how the Bank plans and monitors its transition.

1.2 The Policy implements the ECB Guide on climate-related and environmental risks (2020), EBA/GL/2025/01 (Guidelines on the management of ESG risks), applicable to the Bank from 11 January 2026, and EBA/GL/2025/04 (Guidelines on environmental scenario analysis), and gives effect to the requirement of Directive 2013/36/EU as amended by Directive (EU) 2024/1619 (CRD) to maintain plans addressing the financial risks arising from the transition towards a climate-neutral economy.

## 2 Scope and applicability

2.1 The Policy applies to all Group entities, business lines and risk types. Social and governance risks are addressed in separate policies; environmental risks other than climate (such as biodiversity loss and pollution) are within scope of the materiality assessment in §5.

2.2 Models and quantitative methods used to measure C&E risks (including transition and physical risk models, climate stress test models and climate adjustments to credit risk models) are models under MRM-POL-001 and are subject to §11 of this Policy.

## 3 Definitions

- **Transition risk** — the risk of financial loss arising from the process of adjustment towards a lower-carbon and more environmentally sustainable economy, including policy, technology and market changes.
- **Physical risk** — the risk of financial loss arising from acute (for example floods, storms) or chronic (for example subsidence, sea-level rise, drought) climate and environmental hazards.
- **Materiality assessment** — the periodic process by which the Bank determines which C&E risk drivers have a material impact on its risk categories over the defined time horizons.
- **Transmission channel** — the causal chain through which a C&E risk driver affects counterparties, collateral or markets and thereby the Bank's traditional risk categories.
- **Energy label** — the energy performance certificate of a building (label classes A++++ to G in the Netherlands), as registered in the national register.
- **Financed emissions** — the greenhouse gas emissions of counterparties attributed to the Bank in proportion to its financing.
- **Prudential transition plan** — the plan required under the CRD and specified in EBA/GL/2025/01 describing how the Bank monitors and addresses the financial risks arising from the transition.

## 4 Governance

4.1 The Management Board is responsible for the C&E risk strategy, approves this Policy, the materiality assessment and the prudential transition plan, and oversees C&E risks within the risk appetite framework.

4.2 Sustainability Risk, as part of the second line of defence, owns this Policy, performs the materiality assessment, sets C&E key risk indicators and provides independent challenge on C&E risk integration in the first line.

4.3 C&E risks that are material under §5 shall be reflected in the risk appetite statement through key risk indicators with limits or thresholds, monitored at least quarterly and reported to the Enterprise Risk Committee.

## 5 Materiality assessment

5.1 The Bank shall perform a C&E materiality assessment at least annually and whenever a material change in its business model or in the external environment occurs.

5.2 The assessment shall cover transition and physical risk drivers and their impact on credit, market, operational, liquidity and funding, reputational and business model risk.

5.3 The assessment shall consider a short-term horizon of up to three years, a medium-term horizon of three to ten years and a long-term horizon of at least ten years.

5.4 The assessment shall combine exposure-based methods (such as sectoral and geographical concentration analysis), portfolio-based methods (such as alignment metrics) and scenario-based methods, using both quantitative and qualitative information. Methods, thresholds and data limitations shall be documented.

5.5 A conclusion that a C&E risk is not material shall be justified and documented, including the evidence and the thresholds applied.

5.6 The outcome of the materiality assessment shall be approved by the Management Board and shall feed the ICAAP risk inventory, the stress testing programme under ERM-STD-300 and the model development plan.

## 6 Integration in credit risk management

6.1 C&E risk factors shall be considered in credit origination and in the periodic review of corporate and SME clients, in line with EBA/GL/2020/06, including an assessment of the counterparty's exposure to transition and physical risk and of its transition plan.

6.2 Collateral valuation for residential and commercial real estate shall take into account the energy label and the physical risk exposure of the property.

6.3 For each credit risk model (IRB, IFRS 9 and credit acceptance), model owners shall assess whether material C&E risk drivers are captured by the model's risk drivers and calibration data. The assessment shall be documented in the model documentation and repeated at each model review.

6.4 Where a material C&E risk driver is not captured by a credit risk model, the model owner shall propose a remediation: inclusion of the driver in the model, a documented adjustment, or an overlay in accordance with FIN-STD-200 §10 for IFRS 9 models.

6.5 IFRS 9 SICR assessment and forward-looking scenarios shall consider C&E risk drivers where they are expected to affect credit risk within the lifetime of the exposures.

6.6 Sector policies with exclusion criteria and concentration limits shall be maintained for the carbon-intensive sectors identified as material in §5.

## 7 Integration in the ICAAP

7.1 Material C&E risks shall be included in the ICAAP risk inventory as drivers of existing risk categories.

7.2 In the economic perspective, the Bank shall quantify internal capital for material C&E risks, either within the existing risk-category models or through a dedicated add-on methodology, and shall document the approach.

7.3 In the normative perspective, the adverse scenario shall include C&E risk drivers where they are material over the ICAAP horizon.

## 8 Climate stress testing and scenario analysis

8.1 The Bank shall perform climate stress testing and scenario analysis at least annually, in accordance with ERM-STD-300 §10 and EBA/GL/2025/04.

8.2 The analysis shall include at least an orderly transition, a disorderly transition and a hot-house-world scenario, and shall cover both transition and physical risk.

8.3 Scenario analysis shall identify the transmission channels to the Bank's portfolios, in particular energy-label-driven changes in residential collateral values and borrower affordability, carbon-price-driven changes in corporate profitability and PD, and physical damage to collateral.

8.4 Results shall be used in the materiality assessment, the risk appetite framework, the ICAAP and the prudential transition plan, and shall be reported to the Management Board.

## 9 Data

9.1 The Bank shall collect, for all residential and commercial real estate collateral, the registered energy label from the national register. Where no registered label exists, an estimated label shall be used, flagged as such, and the estimation method documented and validated.

9.2 The coverage and quality of energy label data shall be monitored quarterly, including the share of registered versus estimated labels per portfolio.

9.3 Financed emissions shall be estimated in accordance with a recognised industry methodology for financed emissions accounting, covering Scope 1 and 2 emissions of counterparties and Scope 3 emissions for sectors where they are material. Each estimate shall carry a data quality score.

9.4 Physical risk shall be assessed using address-level or asset-level geolocation data for flood, subsidence, drought and extreme weather hazards.

9.5 Where proxies or estimates are used for missing C&E data, the method shall be documented, the share of proxied data reported, and the uncertainty reflected in risk measurement.

9.6 C&E data used in models shall comply with MRM-STD-022 and DATA-POL-001, including lineage and data quality controls.

9.7 Counterparty-level C&E data (emissions, transition plans, physical asset locations) shall be collected at origination and updated at least at each annual credit review for corporate clients.

## 10 Transition planning

10.1 The Bank shall maintain a prudential transition plan in accordance with the CRD and EBA/GL/2025/01, setting out quantified targets and intermediate milestones for managing the financial risks from the transition, consistent with the Bank's sustainability disclosures.

10.2 The plan shall be supported by forward-looking metrics, such as sectoral alignment metrics and the distribution of energy labels in the mortgage portfolio, monitored at least annually against milestones.

10.3 Deviations from milestones shall be analysed and reported to the Management Board with proposed corrective actions.

## 11 Models

11.1 C&E models shall be registered in the model inventory and tiered according to MRM-STD-010, and validated under MV-STD-001 before being used for ICAAP, stress testing or financial reporting.

11.2 Model documentation shall describe methodological uncertainty specific to C&E risks, including long horizons, the absence of historical loss data and dependence on scenario assumptions, and the sensitivity of results to key assumptions.

11.3 Expert judgement used in C&E models, for example in translating scenario variables into PD and LGD shocks, shall be documented and approved in accordance with MRM-STD-023.

## 12 Roles and responsibilities

12.1 **Management Board** approves this Policy, the materiality assessment and the prudential transition plan.
12.2 **Sustainability Risk (2LoD)** owns the Policy and the materiality assessment and challenges first-line integration.
12.3 **Business lines and Credit Risk Management (1LoD)** integrate C&E factors in origination, review and collateral valuation.
12.4 **Sustainability Risk Modelling (1LoD)** develops C&E models; **Enterprise Stress Testing** runs climate stress tests.
12.5 **Chief Data Office** is responsible for C&E data sourcing and quality.
12.6 **Model Validation (2LoD)** validates C&E models; **Internal Audit (3LoD)** reviews the C&E framework.

## 13 Exceptions and dispensations

13.1 Deviations from this Policy require a dispensation approved by the Enterprise Risk Committee and, where a model provision is concerned, by the Model Risk Committee. Dispensations are time-bound (maximum 12 months) and reported to the Management Board annually.
